Question 2 reuses the Question 1 transformation function. It includes reference
patches and histograms to explain the selected bands, both transformation
curves, image comparisons, and parameter checks. These intensity-only results
highlight tissues; they are not labeled tissue segmentations.

Question 3 changes only the Lab lightness channel, compares three gamma values,
and selects a direct exponent of 0.7. Its histograms show Lab lightness and
separate RGB channels before and after correction. It distinguishes the
intended lightness mapping from the result after conversion back to RGB.

Question 4 splits the image into HSV planes, applies the specified formula to
saturation, compares three values of a, and selects a = 0.5 with sigma = 70.
It shows the original, enhanced image, and transformation curve, and explains
the formula's effect on neutral pixels and the limits of preserving HSV value.

Question 5 implements histogram equalization using histogram counts,
cumulative counts, and a lookup table. It includes a worked example, before
and after images and histograms, CDF comparisons, and checks against known
cases. OpenCV's equalizeHist is used only as a validation reference.

Question 6 shows the HSV channels, raw and filled masks, extracted foreground
and background, selected-region histograms/CDFs, and the recombined result.
It checks that masked histogram counts exclude artificial background zeros
and that background pixels stay identical. The lecture slides referenced by
Question 6 were not supplied; CDF-min normalization is stated explicitly and
still needs to be compared with the exact slide convention.
